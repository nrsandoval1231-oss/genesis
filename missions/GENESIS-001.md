# GENESIS-001 — Next-Generation Distributed Generation

## Mission
Investigate whether a novel or hybrid architecture can materially improve the economics, performance, and deployment characteristics of 100–500 kW distributed generation, initially emphasizing natural-gas-compatible solutions.

## Evaluation dimensions
Net electrical efficiency, fuel consumption, power density, transient response, reliability, maintenance, installed cost, emissions, manufacturability, and commercial feasibility.

## Approach
Compare conventional gas engines and generator sets against credible alternative thermodynamic, mechanical, electrical, and hybrid architectures. Require system-boundary energy accounting, realistic parasitic loads, and equivalent duty-cycle assumptions.

## Existing early experiment — E02
Prior Argus work evaluated nine cases with a pinned Polytrope model. Reported controller cycle time was 0.045225 s (within stated tolerance but above the target), with magnetic energy retention and recovery recorded separately. **Every simulated case consumed net energy.** This is not evidence of a net-positive-energy generator or a physical prototype.

Original artifacts, parameter files, and independent reproductions must be linked before treating numerical results as verified in this repository.

## Next research milestone
1. Establish conventional 100–500 kW performance and cost baselines.
2. Catalog candidate architectures and first-principles constraints.
3. Rank hypotheses by testability, potential value, and model credibility.
4. Reproduce E02 with pinned source and explicit energy ledger.
5. Publish a falsifiable experiment plan for the strongest candidate.

## Current status
Mission defined; early modeling evidence reported; no demonstrated invention, independently verified performance breakthrough, or hardware prototype.
